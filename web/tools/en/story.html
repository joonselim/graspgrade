Key experiments

1Two questions

  • Test 1, contact timing. From first-person video alone, find the moment a hand touches an object and the moment it lets go. The ground truth is tactile glove data that measures palm pressure in the OpenGraph dataset.
  • Test 2, grasps a robot can copy. Score each grasp clip for whether a robot hand could copy that grasp. The ground truth comes from moving the motion capture hand in the HOT3D dataset onto an Allegro robot hand and checking whether the robot hand can make the same grasp.

2Test 1: when contact starts and ends

SAM rule: back-to-back contacts are not split correctly

  • A rule that calls it contact when the hand and object SAM masks overlap and move together. Because the hand is always next to some object, it joins back-to-back contacts into one or cuts them into too many pieces (EXP-14, EXP-22, EXP-24).
  • The final rule, chosen on 50 tuning episodes, still makes 2.5 times too many boundaries, with boundary F1 ±5 of 0.153 and contact IoU of 0.683 (EXP-32).

Muse Spark alone: calls contact early and misses short releases

  • Muse Spark calls the start of contact about 7 frames (0.23 s) earlier than the glove and the end 7 frames later. Learning this difference from the glove data and shifting by it doubles boundary F1 (EXP-29).
  • It almost never notices a hand that lets go for less than 1 s and grasps again. One prompt revision did not help (EXP-30).

Held-out result: Muse Spark plus the glove learned shift is best

  • On 22 held-out episodes, Muse Spark alone plus the timing shift learned from the glove is best on all three metrics: boundary F1 ±5 0.195, ±15 0.376, contact IoU 0.850. The shift helps both methods that use Muse Spark (EXP-37).
  • Adding a Muse Spark boundary check to the SAM rule helps a little (+0.022), but the SAM rule makes too many boundaries, so it stays below Muse Spark alone.

3Test 2: grasps a robot hand can copy

Ground truth: the first label measured the transfer, not the robot

  • The first ground truth label stretched the human hand to the size of the robot hand. In the process the robot fingers lifted off the object, so even a firmly held can was judged "not copyable". The label measured a side effect of the transfer, not a limit of the robot (EXP-10, EXP-15).
  • We switched to a v2 label that puts the robot fingers at the human finger positions and checks whether the robot hand goes into the object (collision) and whether its fingers reach where the human touched (reach). 47% of clips are "copyable", with no link to human hand size (EXP-16, EXP-20).

What decides it: the object, not video quality

  • Knowing only which object it is predicts the label with AUC 0.74. Video quality items such as camera shake add only 0.01 to that (EXP-17).
  • So the checklist moved from video quality to questions about the object: size relative to the hand, shape, thickness, wrap width, handle and free space (EXP-18, EXP-19).

Checklist: 12 questions per clip give the score

Grasp clipA few seconds of video from just before the hand touches the object to just after it lets go
12 questions7 to Muse Spark, 2 from SAM masks, 3 computed from video. Each is answered high, mid or low
Grades to scoreHigh 1, mid 0.5, low 0. Summed with learned weights into 0 to 100
CheckAUC against the robot hand ground truth label

Held-out result: the checklist lifts Muse Spark from chance

  • On 1,426 held-out clips, asking Muse Spark "can a robot hand copy this" in one go gives AUC 0.501, which is chance. Splitting it into the checklist questions raises it to 0.624. The gain comes from splitting the question, and learning the weights adds almost nothing (EXP-40).
  • It holds at 0.571 on objects that were not in training. But for known objects the object name alone gives a higher 0.771, and our score cannot rank grasps of the same object either, so the score ranks objects rather than grasps.
  • The result holds when the depth the robot hand may go into the object in the ground truth is raised from 5 mm to 10 mm, but drops to chance with a strict 2 mm (EXP-41).

4Scope: rigid objects

  • The score is for rigid objects. All 33 HOT3D objects in Test 2 are tracked as rigid bodies, and in the labels of two people only 5 squeeze bottles and cartons are squeezable and the rest are rigid.
  • To use it on video with soft objects, those need to be filtered out first. Asking Muse Spark whether the object is soft held back 51.7% of soft object segments, below the 70% rule set in advance, and mostly missed tape. Only 2.1% of rigid HOT3D clips were held back by mistake (EXP-42).
  • Leaving out the held-back HOT3D clips keeps the Test 2 AUC the same, 0.624 to 0.625.

5Rules and formulas

Test 1 ground truth: cleaned glove

ct = 1[ Σi=1..80 pi,t > 800 ]80 pressure sensors per hand, 30 fps
  • Gaps of 0.1 s (3 frames) or less are joined, and pieces shorter than 0.1 s are dropped (EXP-14).

Test 1 method B: SAM mask rule (10 fps)

neart = [ dt ≤ τd ]dt: distance in pixels between the hand mask and the nearest object mask
mt = max( ‖Δot−3→t‖, ‖Δot→t+3‖ ) / √Aoobject motion in units of object size
κt = ‖Δo − Δh‖ / ( ‖Δo‖ + ‖Δh‖ + 5 )near 0 means hand and object move together
movet = neart ∧ handt ∧ mt ≥ τm ∧ κt ≤ τκ
contact segment = a near run that contains movet in at least one frame
  • Δo and Δh are the motion of the object and hand mask centers with the camera motion removed. The camera motion is the median motion of the other objects over the same span. Ao is the median object mask area.
  • Gaps of f frames or less are joined and segments shorter than 0.2 s are dropped. A segment is cut when the nearest object changes and the new one stays for q frames or more (EXP-22, EXP-24).
  • Thresholds come from a grid search on tuning episodes: (τd, τm, τκ, f, q) = argmax [ F1±15 + IoU ], τd ∈ {2, 4, 8, 12} px, τm ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}, τκ ∈ {0.5, 0.7}, f ∈ {0, 1, 2}, q ∈ {1, 3, 5}.
  • Values chosen on 50 tuning episodes: τd = 2 px, τm = 0.05, τκ = 0.7, f = 0, q = 3, shift δs = +4, δe = −1 frame (EXP-32).

Test 1 timing shift (all three methods)

δs = −median( ŝ − s ),  δe = −median( ê − e )
ŝ′ = ŝ + δs,  ê′ = ê + δe
  • ŝ and ê are the predicted contact start and end, s and e the glove start and end. The medians are computed on tuning episodes.
  • For Muse Spark alone, δs = +7 and δe = −7 frames. Boundary F1 ±5 rises from 0.06 to 0.13 (EXP-29). Method C learns only from tuning boundaries that Muse Spark moved, δs = +6 and δe = −4 frames (EXP-36).

Test 1 metrics

Predicted and glove boundaries are matched one to one within |ŝ − s| ≤ k
P = matches / predicted boundaries,  R = matches / glove boundaries,  F1±k = 2PR / (P + R)
IoU = |predicted contact frames ∩ glove contact frames| / |predicted contact frames ∪ glove contact frames|
  • k = 5 and 15 frames (0.17 s and 0.5 s).

Test 2 ground truth: v2 label

copyable = ∃ u ∈ U : grip(u) ∧ reach(u) ∧ ¬collide(u)U: shifts of the whole hand, 1 cm grid, within 2 cm
grip: the Allegro thumb tip and at least one other fingertip are within 1.5 cm of the object surface
reach: for every human finger that touches the object, the matching Allegro fingertip is within 2 cm
collide: some point on the Allegro hand surface is more than 5 mm inside the object
  • The Allegro hand is fitted to the positions of the motion capture fingertips. The thresholds were fixed before computing (EXP-16).

Test 2 item grades

g(x) = 1 (x ≥ q2/3),  0.5 (q1/3 ≤ x < q2/3),  0 (x < q1/3)q: tertiles on tuning clips
  • Camera stability: x = −(90th percentile of camera motion during the clip).
  • Hand shape: x = logistic regression probability on 10 MediaPipe hand features (trained on the tuning training clips).
  • Visibility: x = share of frames where SAM finds both the hand and the object.
  • Contact clarity: x = share of contact frames with dt ≤ 3 px minus the same share in reach and release frames.
  • Motion completeness (fixed rule): g = ½( [hand visible ≥ 50% in the 1 s reach ∧ wrist moves ≥ 3% of the frame] + [the same in the 1 s release] ).
  • Seven Muse Spark items (finger occlusion, size relative to the hand, shape, thickness, wrap width, handle, free space): the three level answer becomes 1, 0.5 or 0.

Test 2 score and comparisons

S = 100 · σ( w0 + Σi wi gi ),  σ(z) = 1 / (1 + e−z)our score, all items
R1 = the "copyable" probability Muse Spark answers
R2 = (1/7) Σi∈Muse giplain average of the Muse grades, no learning
R3 = σ( w0 + Σi∈Muse wi gi )same grades, learned weights
r(o) = "copyable" rate of object o on tuning clipsobject name baseline
AUC = P( S+ > S− ) + ½ P( S+ = S− )
  • w is learned on 400 tuning clips with L2 logistic regression (C = 0.5). 95% intervals come from a bootstrap over sequences.
  • Unseen objects: for each object o, the hand shape model, tertiles and w are learned again on clips without o, and then the clips of o are scored. The object name baseline then gives every clip the same value, so its AUC is 0.5.

6Public benchmark: score video selection against sensors

Training robots on human video needs a rule that picks the segments to use. This benchmark compares the segments or scores such a rule produces with ground truth built from tactile gloves and motion capture, and returns a score.
